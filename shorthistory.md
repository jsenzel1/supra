  <p style="font-family: Baskerville" source align="center">
  this is the very first supra prototype: a raspberry pi pico, a display, a joystick, and 2 jacks on a piece of 50 cent perfboard. This lasted a long while. I spent the first couple weeks of work on supra obsessing and slaving over getting the u8g2 library to work in c++ instead of c. I was convinced that some small extra setup steps to using the C library each time would prove to be massively annoying. I think this was driven by feeling like I could have done things better with some earlier on setup of tools/code pipeline on my previous module Pet Rock. It ended up being a crazy wild goose chase. I spent so much time on it, then just opened a github issue where the dev said, no that's not supported, and then I used the C version anyway. It was totally fine, wasted a lot of time thinking I needed to do something the "right way"
  </p>

  <p source align="center">
   some imaginings of the front panel. Most of my designs of supra included another meta layer of use which would involve modifying the modes over time, or aquiring new ones
    this one is my phase of having a "shop" where you would accumulate points while using the module and spend them on mutations and upgrades of the mode. I still
    am really enamored with this idea, but It didn't end up fitting in very well. I could never nail down exactly what you would buy etc. I feel like if I had put
    in more work I could have figured it out, and I still want to make something like that sometime. Another huge idea was having an overworld with a character that 
    would move around and do things - there was going to be a pirate trader ship that each sunday would bring new modes that you could swap with ones in your inventory
    to use for a while. This meta trading system was really based on the idea that there would be 10+ modes, and that they would be simpler and more combinatorial. With 
    the 4 I ended up with it just didn't make sense. Cutting that was the hardest thing in development, because it was one of the things I was most excited by. I think 
    now after having to explain the 4 modes to everyone as they confusidly ponder supra, that it would have been incredibly difficult to 
    explain such an incredibly complex module to people. But I don't really make things to be easily understandable, I want them to be different and deep and experimental. 
    so that is still my greatest dissapointment with supra, that I missed that extra level of depth and complexity. But that's been a legitimate point of learning about
    how I scope and design. Pet Rock was so straight forward: I had to make design decisions, but not many. I was mostly executing on my original vision. Supra was so much 
    more open ended, its helped me see how I would change my design methodology in the future. I'm still very proud of the module and how things came out, and I have so much 
    fun using it myself, which is really the thing that matters most to me. 
  </p>

 <p source align="center">
   
   another side project of the project that never made it was resin 3d printing the topper for the joystick. I was going to do a bunch of crazy and interesting shapes. Resin 3d printing is an incredible technology and allows you to print one off parts in super high precision to the point where they look industrially made. But they are brittle, etc. etc. The foray i did was fun but I started to realize that getting it right was going to be a major endeavor and take way too much work for something that would be an "oh neat" moment, not a core functionality of the device

 I planned to go to superbooth for a long time. I found the only way I actually finished Pet Rock was by setting a date to exhibit it (at knobcon) so I did the same. 
    turned out this time it took another 9 months of random delays, some my fault, some not. That has been very frustrating because mentally I was going to be done with the
    project nine months before writing this, and everything stretched out crazily. Superbooth itself was a mad dash to finish. I had a package of circuit boards come in so 
    close that i redirected it to berlin, and I had no soldering equipment to assemble them. I couldn't find one hackerspace open that would allow me to use any tools. So 
    I ended up soldering everything on the balcony of my friend I was staying with with the cheapest possible tools from a hardware store. It was a beautiful day. At one point
    a bag of extremely tiny hard to get lightpipes blew off the balcony and I rand down to the street barefoot to try and find them. A nice shop owner helped me spy them on top of an awning and get them down. I had finished the code during an insane fever dream of jet lag at a berlin library before taking a nap in a park
  </p>

<p source align="center">
it's hard to be launching what feels like so late. What keeps me sane is seeing other makers struggle in the same ways. Parts go out of stock, 2 more weeks. Parts go out of stock again, 2 more weeks. Endless to do lists of busy and technical work that's totally apart from any job or organizational system. The fun part is completely over, and then you have to force yourself to do pure logistics until its out!!! so I'm chalking it up to another learning experience. And there's only been a few moments of agony lol. I've actually had soooooo much fun making supra. It's been a true true labor of love :) I hope it brings a little joy to someone else too </p>
